# Research Question

## Project

**Self-Supervised Discovery of Zebrafish Behavioral Structure**

---

# 1. Primary Research Question

> **Do self-supervised temporal representations of zebrafish behavior reveal reproducible behavioral structure that is not captured by conventional hand-engineered locomotion and pose features?**

This is the primary question the project is designed to answer.

The study will compare two representations of the **same temporal behavioral windows**:

```text
Input A
Hand-Engineered Locomotion / Pose Features

vs.

Input B
Self-Supervised Temporal Embeddings
```

Both representations will be evaluated using comparable unsupervised behavioral-discovery procedures.

The purpose of the comparison is not merely to determine which representation produces visually cleaner clusters. The project must determine whether the learned representation contains **additional, reproducible behavioral information** after controlling for major alternative explanations.

---

# 2. Operational Research Question

For the purposes of experimentation, the primary question is operationalized as:

> Given identical temporal windows of zebrafish behavior, does an unsupervised discovery pipeline operating on self-supervised temporal embeddings recover stable behavioral structure across held-out animals that cannot be fully reconstructed or explained using hand-engineered locomotion and pose features?

This formulation establishes several requirements.

A positive result must involve:

- the same underlying behavioral observations,
- separate baseline and learned representations,
- comparable discovery procedures,
- held-out fish,
- quantitative comparison,
- reproducibility,
- and controls for nuisance variables.

---

# 4. Input A — Baseline Representation

Input A represents conventional manually defined behavioral measurements.

Candidate features include:

## Locomotion

- instantaneous speed,
- mean speed,
- maximum speed,
- acceleration,
- distance traveled,
- movement duration,
- immobility,
- movement bout duration,
- inter-bout intervals.

## Turning

- heading,
- angular velocity,
- turning angle,
- turning frequency,
- trajectory curvature.

## Pose

Where available:

- body orientation,
- body curvature,
- tail curvature,
- tail-beat amplitude,
- tail-beat frequency,
- relative keypoint angles,
- pose velocity.

## Temporal Summary Statistics

Within each behavioral window:

- mean,
- standard deviation,
- median,
- minimum,
- maximum,
- quantiles,
- derivatives,
- temporal variability,
- frequency-domain measurements where justified.

The final feature set will be frozen before confirmatory testing.

---

# 5. Input B — Learned Representation

Input B consists of embeddings generated by a self-supervised temporal representation model.

The SSL system will learn representations without requiring manually assigned behavioral labels.

Potential model inputs include:

- video,
- cropped fish video,
- pose sequences,
- trajectory sequences,
- or combinations of these.

For every behavioral window:

```text
Behavioral Window
        │
        ▼
Self-Supervised Encoder
        │
        ▼
Learned Embedding
```

For window `i`:

```text
Window_i → z_i
```

where `z_i` is the learned representation of that behavioral sequence.

---

# 6. Unit of Analysis

The primary unit of analysis is:

> **A fixed-duration temporal behavioral window belonging to one identifiable zebrafish.**

Example:

```text
Fish
 │
 ├── Window 001
 ├── Window 002
 ├── Window 003
 └── ...
```

Each window represents a short continuous period of behavior.

Candidate durations include:

- 0.5 seconds,
- 1 second,
- 2 seconds,
- 4 seconds.

One primary duration will be selected for the main analysis.

Additional durations will be used for sensitivity testing.

---

# 7. Experimental Population

The project will use existing authorized zebrafish behavioral data.

The preferred primary dataset should contain:

- multiple fish,
- identifiable animals,
- temporal behavioral recordings,
- sufficient recording duration,
- usable tracking or pose information,
- and enough metadata to evaluate nuisance variables.

The research question concerns **behavioral representation**, not a particular genotype, disease model, treatment, or experimental stimulus.

Any conclusions must therefore remain within the biological population represented by the selected dataset.

---

# 8. Primary Hypothesis

## H1 — Additional Behavioral Structure

> Self-supervised temporal embeddings will reveal reproducible behavioral structure that is not fully captured by the predefined hand-engineered locomotion and pose feature representation.

This is the central hypothesis.

Evidence for H1 requires more than demonstrating that SSL produces clusters.

The learned structure must survive comparison against the baseline.

---

# 9. Null Hypothesis

## H0

> After controlling for locomotor, pose, identity, session, tracking, and segmentation effects, self-supervised temporal representations will not reveal reproducible behavioral structure beyond that represented by hand-engineered behavioral features.

Evidence consistent with H0 may include:

- SSL clusters are highly predictable from baseline features,
- SSL clusters primarily reflect swimming speed,
- learned clusters are unstable,
- learned clusters encode individual fish,
- learned clusters encode recording sessions,
- apparent clusters arise from tracking errors,
- or learned structure disappears on held-out animals.

Failure to reject this hypothesis is an acceptable research outcome.

---

# 10. Secondary Research Questions

## RQ2 — Reproducibility

> Are behavioral structures identified from the self-supervised representation reproducible across independently held-out zebrafish?

The central concern is whether a discovered state represents a recurring behavioral pattern rather than a peculiarity of the animals used during model development.

---

## RQ3 — Cluster Stability

> Are behavioral states discovered from self-supervised embeddings stable across repeated model training and clustering runs?

Stability should be evaluated across:

- random seeds,
- model initialization,
- resampling,
- reasonable clustering configurations.

---

## RQ4 — Beyond-Speed Structure

> Does the self-supervised representation capture behavioral organization beyond locomotor speed?

This is a critical validity question.

A representation that primarily rediscovered:

```text
slow
medium
fast
```

would not provide strong evidence for additional behavioral structure.

---

## RQ5 — Identity Independence

> Is the learned behavioral organization distinguishable from individual fish identity?

The experiment should determine whether embeddings primarily represent:

```text
"What is the fish doing?"
```

rather than:

```text
"Which fish is this?"
```

---

## RQ6 — Session Independence

> Is learned behavioral structure reproducible across recording sessions rather than being driven by session-specific visual or experimental conditions?

Potential nuisance variables include:

- camera,
- tank,
- lighting,
- background,
- compression,
- recording date.

---

## RQ7 — Tracking Robustness

> Are discovered behavioral states robust to tracking quality, or are apparent states caused by pose/tracking failures?

Potential artifact clusters may arise from:

- missing keypoints,
- coordinate jumps,
- occlusion,
- motion blur,
- bad detections.

---

## RQ8 — Window Robustness

> Are discovered states robust to reasonable changes in temporal window size and window alignment?

Behavior should not depend entirely on arbitrary segmentation boundaries.

---

## RQ9 — Baseline Recoverability

> To what extent can behavioral states discovered in the learned representation be reconstructed from hand-engineered features?

One possible experiment is:

```text
Hand-Engineered Features
          │
          ▼
     Classifier
          │
          ▼
Predict SSL Cluster Membership
```

High predictive performance would suggest that SSL clusters largely represent information already contained in the baseline.

Incomplete recoverability would provide evidence that SSL captures additional information.

---

# 14. Primary Validity Threats

The project must explicitly design against six central threats.

## Threat 1 — Identity Leakage

The model clusters individual fish instead of behavior.

### Key Test

Can fish identity be predicted from embeddings?

---

## Threat 2 — Session / Camera Leakage

The model clusters recording conditions instead of behavior.

### Key Test

Can session identity be predicted from embeddings or cluster membership?

---

## Threat 3 — Speed-Only Solution

The learned representation primarily reconstructs locomotor intensity.

### Key Test

Does learned structure remain within speed-matched samples?

---

## Threat 4 — Tracking Artifacts

Apparent behaviors correspond to pose-estimation failures.

### Key Test

Are cluster assignments associated with tracking confidence or tracking errors?

---

## Threat 5 — Window-Boundary Artifacts

Clusters depend on arbitrary temporal segmentation.

### Key Test

Do comparable states remain when windows are shifted or resized?

---

## Threat 6 — Hyperparameter Fishing

An apparently meaningful result is produced only after trying many configurations.

### Key Control

Predefine:

- primary pipeline,
- primary metrics,
- primary clustering method,
- main window length,
- allowable sensitivity analyses.

---

# 15. Primary Comparison Tests

The study should answer the research question using several complementary tests.

## Test A — Discovery Comparison

Run unsupervised discovery separately on:

```text
Baseline Features
```

and:

```text
SSL Embeddings
```

Compare the resulting organization.

---

## Test B — Stability

Repeat discovery under multiple seeds.

Question:

> Does approximately the same behavioral organization reappear?

---

## Test C — Held-Out Fish

Train/develop using one set of animals.

Evaluate using fish not used for model development.

Question:

> Does the discovered organization generalize to new fish?

---

## Test D — Baseline Recoverability

Use baseline features to predict SSL-derived state assignments.

Question:

> Can conventional features reproduce what SSL discovered?

---

## Test E — Speed Control

Measure the relationship between:

```text
SSL Representation
```

and:

```text
Speed
```

Then compare behavior within speed-matched observations.

---

## Test F — Nuisance Prediction

Attempt to predict:

```text
Fish ID
Session ID
Camera ID
```

where available.

Strong nuisance predictability would weaken behavioral claims.

---

## Test G — Tracking Quality

Determine whether discovered states correspond disproportionately to low-quality tracking.

---

## Test H — Window Sensitivity

Repeat discovery under alternate:

- window lengths,
- window offsets,
- window overlaps.

# 16. Evidence Required for the Primary Claim

The target claim is:

> **Self-supervised temporal representations reveal reproducible zebrafish behavioral structure not fully captured by the evaluated hand-engineered feature set.**

This claim requires evidence that:

- [ ] meaningful structure appears in SSL embeddings,
- [ ] the structure is reproducible across random seeds,
- [ ] it appears in held-out fish,
- [ ] it is not dominated by fish identity,
- [ ] it is not dominated by recording session,
- [ ] it cannot be explained solely by speed,
- [ ] it is not caused primarily by tracking failures,
- [ ] it is robust to reasonable window changes,
- [ ] and baseline features cannot completely reproduce it.

---

# 17. Evidence That Is Not Sufficient

The following observations alone will **not** support the primary claim.

## A Visually Attractive UMAP

Example:

```text
Several clearly separated colored islands
```

Visual separation is exploratory evidence only.

---

## A High Silhouette Score

High geometric cluster separation does not establish biological relevance.

---

## Many Clusters

More clusters do not imply richer or more accurate behavioral structure.

---

## SSL Outperforming One Arbitrary Baseline

The baseline must be reasonably representative of conventional zebrafish behavioral analysis.

---

## Clusters Correlated With Speed

This may indicate that SSL has simply rediscovered locomotor intensity.

---

## Cluster Differences Between Fish

This may indicate identity leakage rather than behavior.

---

# 18. Claim Levels

## Level 0 — Negative Result

Evidence indicates:

- unstable structure,
- nuisance-driven clusters,
- or no meaningful SSL advantage.

Allowed conclusion:

> The evaluated self-supervised representation did not reveal reliable behavioral structure beyond the hand-engineered baseline under the tested conditions.

---

## Level 1 — Useful Representation

Evidence indicates:

- stable embeddings,
- reasonable cross-animal generalization,
- improvement on predefined representation metrics.

Allowed conclusion:

> Self-supervised learning provides a useful representation of zebrafish behavioral dynamics.

---

## Level 2 — Additional Behavioral Structure

Evidence indicates:

- stable SSL-derived behavioral states,
- held-out fish reproducibility,
- successful nuisance controls,
- incomplete recoverability by baseline features,
- interpretable temporal or movement differences.

Allowed conclusion:

> Self-supervised temporal representations reveal reproducible behavioral structure not fully captured by the evaluated hand-engineered locomotion and pose features.

**This is the target contribution.**

---

## Level 3 — Candidate Novel Behavior

This requires substantially stronger evidence, including:

- replication,
- literature comparison,
- biological interpretation,
- independent animals,
- artifact rejection,
- preferably independent datasets,
- and ideally expert validation.

Allowed conclusion:

> A reproducible learned state represents a candidate previously under-characterized behavioral pattern.

The initial project should not assume it will reach this level.

---

# 19. What This Study Does Not Claim

The project does **not** begin with the claim that:

> Zebrafish possess unknown behaviors that SSL will discover.

Instead, it asks whether additional behavioral organization exists that is not captured by the selected conventional representation.

The project also does not initially claim:

- discovery of a new biological behavior,
- discovery of a neurological mechanism,
- diagnosis of disease,
- evidence of zebrafish emotion,
- causal biological explanations,
- superiority of all SSL methods over all handcrafted methods.

---

# 20. Expected Contribution

The contribution is methodological and empirical.

The intended contribution is to determine whether:

```text
Self-Supervised Temporal Learning
            │
            ▼
captures behavioral information
            │
            ▼
beyond manually specified
locomotion / pose features
```

under a controlled, reproducible comparison.

A strong negative result would also be useful because it would demonstrate that conventional behavioral variables explain most of the structure learned by the tested SSL approach.

# 21. Pre-Registered Direction of Analysis

The project will not require SSL to "win."

Possible outcomes include:

### Outcome A

```text
SSL > Baseline
```

with robust additional behavioral structure.

Supports the target contribution.

### Outcome B

```text
SSL ≈ Baseline
```

Suggests handcrafted features already capture most relevant structure.

### Outcome C

```text
SSL < Baseline
```

Suggests that the selected SSL method is unsuitable, undertrained, or unnecessary for the dataset.

### Outcome D

```text
SSL clusters nuisance variables
```

Indicates representation failure despite apparently strong cluster separation.

All four outcomes are valid research results.

# 23. Discovery vs Validation

The project explicitly separates:

## Discovery

Question:

> What behavioral structure appears in the representation?

from:

## Validation

Question:

> Is that structure reproducible and genuinely behavioral?

The existence of a cluster is therefore the **beginning of the analysis**, not its conclusion.

## Primary

> **RQ1:** Do self-supervised temporal representations of zebrafish behavior reveal reproducible behavioral structure that is not captured by conventional hand-engineered locomotion and pose features?

## Secondary

> **RQ2:** Does the learned behavioral structure reproduce across held-out individual zebrafish?

> **RQ3:** Is the learned behavioral structure stable across repeated training and discovery runs?

> **RQ4:** Does the learned representation capture behavioral structure beyond locomotor speed?

> **RQ5:** Is the discovered structure independent of individual fish identity?
